The difference

A job has a beginning, a middle and an end.

That is how we design each employee. Not around a prompt box — around the full piece of work someone is currently doing by hand.

01 / DEFINE

Start with the job.

What comes in? What decisions are allowed? What counts as done? Where does a human need to approve?

02 / CONNECT

Give the employee the right context.

Its systems, documents, data, rules and communication style — enough to do useful work without guessing.

03 / EXECUTE

Let it move the work.

Read, decide, send, update, call, reconcile or route — with evidence and clear escalation when something falls outside the rules.

04 / HAND OFF

Then pass the baton.

A good employee knows when the job belongs to another employee — or to a human being.

The thinking

We are not trying to make one AI that does everything.

We think the better model is a small team of specialists. Each one owns a job, has a working personality and knows where its responsibility ends.

Specific beats cleverA narrow job with clear rules is easier to trust and improve.
Evidence beats confidenceShow the source, record the action and surface uncertainty.
Handoffs matterThe real system is the space between jobs, not just the jobs themselves.

FAQ

The useful questions.

Is this just a chatbot with a name?

No. The intended design is a defined job: inputs, rules, tools, actions, records, approvals and a clear handoff.

Do we have to replace our software?

No. The goal is to work around the systems you already use and automate the repetitive work between them.

Do all eight employees have to be built at once?

No. Start with one workflow. The rest are added when there is a real job for them to own.

Where do humans stay involved?

Where judgment, approval, sensitive communication or exceptions require a person. The employee should make that handoff obvious.
